# Elevate 2026 — three aftermovies, three films

Shared ground rules for all three:
- 1920×1080, 30 fps, real photos only.
- Every cut and effect sits on an analysed musical event (beat, downbeat, kick, snare, cymbal, bass hit, vocal word, section change).
- Photos are graded to one film stock per version. Phone shots get grain, halation and film colour so they sit next to the disposable scans; nothing gets cleaned up.
- Faces are detected and used as zoom and pan targets, and nothing crops through a face.
- No two adjacent shots share framing, motion, effect and transition.

Audio cuts are bar-aligned, and I've checked them for harmonic match: chroma similarity is 0.90 or higher at every jump. Times in the tables are *film time*, with the song time in brackets.

## 1. "A MOMENT, A LOVE" — Sweet Disposition (~73 s)
**The bittersweet one. Minimal text, cinematic.**

**Concept:** the story told in the order it happened, as one long memory that ends at the departures board, everyone flying out of Amsterdam. It starts letterboxed and soft, like remembering. When the climax hits, the letterbox opens to full frame: *won't stop 'til it's over*.

**Audio edit**
- Intro: shimmer guitar plus "Sweet disposition" [1.0–15.9]. It starts under a low-pass "underwater" filter that opens over 4 bars.
- The drums land on the cut into the build before "A moment, a love…" [64.3–82.6].
- "And while our blood's still young, it's so young, it runs" [95.6–103.0].
- Into the final "Won't stop 'til it's over / won't stop to surrender" [184.5–199.2], then the last "won't stop" plus the outro ring-out [214.0–231.6].

**Grade:** warm golden film, lifted blacks, consistent across phone and film.
**Text:** title, one lower third, the eight sung words, the departures board, the end card. That's it.

## 2. "THE BAD BANANAS: FATED TO PRETEND" — Time to Pretend (~80 s)
**The jokey one. Lots of captions; rockumentary meets tabloid.**

**Concept:** the friend group is a band, the Bad Bananas, on their "Elevate Tour 2026". MGMT's rock-star fantasy lyrics get hijacked line by line with the most ironic photo possible. That includes the graduate-program wink, *"get jobs in offices"* over the suits, without ever naming the company. It ends on the tour poster for the farewell tour, which is everyone's new city.

**Audio edit:**
- Synth build, the drop and verse 1 [24.1–47.9].
- It cuts before the Paris and heroin lines, straight to "This is our decision…", and runs through to "To pretend" [66.9–~119.5].
- A tape-stop on "pre-tend", then a vinyl-crackle hold over the poster.

## 3. "COOL KIDS / 27 EXPOSURES" — Cool Kids (~69 s)
**The playful one. Medium text; the disposable camera narrates.**

**Concept:** the film *is* the roll of film. It opens on an old film-leader countdown. Maria aims the disposable at us, the flash fires, and every photo after that "is taken": an exposure counter in the corner counts 27 → 1, with an orange LCD date stamp. Structurally it's about **people**: polaroids with handwritten names drop in during the verse. The song's stop-time breaks become freeze-frame gags. At the chorus, *"I wish that I could be like the cool kids"*, the answer arrives in handwriting: *spoiler: you were.* It ends as a contact sheet with fake credits.

**Audio edit:**
- Guitar intro and 4 bars of the band riff [1.5–17.7].
- Verse 1 with both stop-time breaks [25.1–40.0].
- "Yeah, they're invincible… and she says… I wish that I could be like the cool kids…" [47.4–62.4].
- A jump into the final full stop and the last chorus [171.3–187.7].
- The song's actual ending [213.5–220].

| Spine | chronology (places) | lyric gags (band myth) | people (the roll of film) |
